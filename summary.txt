World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Abu Dhabi: US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks ended with agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange, size unsettled; Kushner and Witkoff flew to Moscow, then Dublin. Kyiv's Umierov said big gaps remain (AP).

Iran: a tanker burned after a Hormuz mine strike (AP); Tehran keeps the strait shut until seven conditions, including an end to the US blockade, are met (World Watch). Brent crude fell below $90.

Italy: bomb threats emailed to over 6,300 schools forced 300-plus evacuations; Meloni saw no concrete danger (AP, ANSA). Scotland and Ireland called the emails a hoax.

Spain: millions joined the first general strike in 14 years over housing; unions reported 90% stoppages at ports and car plants (Reuters, AP). Rent averages €1,176, 98.7% of a young worker's pay.

Seoul: the Fair Trade Commission fined 15 banks and brokers a record 1.63 trillion won ($1.22bn) for rigging Treasury auctions (Korea Herald).

Congo: Ebola cases passed 11,000 — 11,042 with 5,387 deaths, government data showed — while Kenya discharged its last patient (AP, WHO).

Gaza: strikes killed at least eight Palestinians, a father and son among them (Democracy Now); Netanyahu denied holding up winter aid as floods hit tents (AP).

Los Angeles: police arrested Ihor Demianenko, 61, on three murder counts after a crash that killed three women by a Metro station (AP).

Ethiopia: residents report over 65 dead, 27 civilians, in an attack on Dalati; police say 52 and blame the Oromo Liberation Army (Addis Standard, France 24).

Washington: a Florida judge let Trump's $10bn libel suit against the BBC proceed, dropping only his trade-practices claim (BBC).

Antalya: COP31's leaders' summit opened as Türkiye pushed a 35% electricity goal for 2035; the Global Carbon Project sees fossil CO2 down 0.4% (Anadolu).

Sport: Stephanie Case became the first woman to finish a nonstop 100-mile race in Antarctica, in 17:31:39 (Anadolu).
